The yields of \bskkg and \lbpkg decays misidentified as \kpig candidates are evaluated using an extended unbinned maximum-likelihood fit performed simultaneously to the \kpig, \kkg and \pkg final states.
To reduce the dependence of the misidentified yield evaluation on the dihadron spectrum of these decays, the requirements on \mkpi are also applied on the invariant mass of the $\Kp\Km$ and $p\Km$ combinations when the pion mass hypothesis is applied to one of the kaons in the \bskkg decay or to the proton.
The yields of the misidentified decays are parametrised as their yield measured in the correct final states multiplied by the selection efficiency ratio.
Selection efficiencies are derived using simulated samples of \mbox{\decay{H_b}{\left[\Km h^+\right]_{X}\gamma}} decays, where $H_b$ is a \bquark-hadron, $h^+$ is a charged pion, kaon or proton and $X$ denotes a resonant state with a large fit fraction.
The efficiencies for the \mbox{\decay{\Bzb}{\left[\Km\pip\right]_X\gamma}} and \mbox{\decay{\Bs}{\left[\Kp\Km\right]_X\gamma}} decays are evaluated as weighted averages of all considered resonant states, where the efficiency for each decay mode is weighted by its fit fraction~\cite{PDG2024,LHCb-PAPER-2024-002}.
The uncertainties on the fit fractions are enlarged by the deviation of the summed fit fractions from unity to account for nonresonant, interference, and neglected contributions.
Since the \mbox{\decay{\Lb}{\left[p\Km\right]_X\gamma}} decay includes a very large number of interfering resonances~\cite{LHCb-PAPER-2023-036}, four resonances of different masses are considered and each assigned equal weight in the average.
When evaluating the systematic uncertainties associated with external inputs, pseudoexperiments are generated using an alternative model that includes only one of the four resonances in turn.
Figure~\ref{fig:datafit_allFS} shows the invariant-mass distribution of candidates selected in each of the three final states summed over the four analysed datasets, as well as the summed fit models.

In the Standard Model (SM), \decay{\bquark}{\dquark\gamma} and \decay{\bquark}{\squark\gamma} quark transitions proceed via electroweak loop diagrams.\footnote{The inclusion of charge-conjugate processes is implied throughout this Letter.}
Particles beyond the SM (BSM) of arbitrarily high mass can contribute to these diagrams~\cite{Li:2013vlx,Xiao:2003vq,Ali:2002kw,Arhrib:2001gr}.
The \decay{\bquark}{\squark\gamma} transition, with amplitude proportional to the Cabibbo--Kobayashi--Maskawa (CKM) matrix element \Vtss,  has been measured through several radiative \bquark-hadron decays~\cite{LHCb-PAPER-2019-015, LHCb-PAPER-2021-030, LHCb-PAPER-2020-020, LHCb-PAPER-2024-030, LHCb-PAPER-2012-019, Belle-II:2024kbc, BaBar:2015chw, Paul:2016urs}.
In contrast, measurements of the \decay{\bquark}{\dquark\gamma} transition, with amplitude proportional to \Vtds, remain limited.
Branching fractions (\BF) of the \decay{\B^{+/0}}{\rho(770)^{+/0}\gamma} and \decay{\Bd}{\omega(782)\gamma} decays have been measured~\cite{LHCb-PAPER-2025-017,Belle:2024qhe,Belle:2008imr,BaBar:2008txc} as well as the inclusive branching fraction of \mbox{\decay{B}{X_{d}\gamma}} decays~\cite{BaBar:2010vgu}, where $X_d$ is an unflavoured hadronic system.

The ratio of branching fractions between decays mediated by \decay{\bquark}{\dquark\gamma} and \decay{\bquark}{\squark\gamma} transitions can be used to measure $|\Vtd/\Vts|$~\cite{Ball:2006nr,Ball:2006eu}.
This value may be compared to determinations based on \BdorBs meson oscillations~\cite{LHCb-PAPER-2021-005,HFLAV23} to identify potential contributions from BSM processes.
The value of $|\Vtd/\Vts|$ extracted from the measured branching fraction ratio \mbox{$\mathcal{R}\equiv\BF(\decay{\Bs}{\Kstarzbv\gamma})/\BF(\decay{\Bzb}{\Kstarzbv\gamma})$} is theoretically cleaner than determinations using other decays involving \decay{\bquark}{\dquark\gamma} transitions since dominant uncertainties from the ratios of form factors largely cancel~\cite{Ball:2006eu}.
Using the existing calculations of the form-factor ratio and nonfactorisable effects~\cite{Ball:2006eu} with updated meson lifetimes, masses and CKM matrix elements~\cite{CKMfitter2005,CKMfitter2015,PDG2024}, the prediction is \mbox{$\mathcal{R}=(3.9\pm0.7)\times10^{-2}$}.
However, a new calculation of the form-factor ratio and nonfactorisable effects, incorporating the latest experimental information, will be necessary to reduce the uncertainty on an eventual measurement of $|\Vtd/\Vts|$ using \bskstg decays.

This Letter reports the first search and first evidence for the \bskpig decay.
The reconstructed $\Km\pip$ system includes contributions from the \Kstarzbv resonance as well as higher-mass states up to 1800\mevcc.
Additionally, the ratio of its branching fraction with respect to the \bdkpig decay is measured.
The analysis uses proton-proton ($pp$) collision data recorded by the LHCb experiment, corresponding to integrated luminosities of $3\invfb$ at centre-of-mass energies of 7 and 8\tev, and $6\invfb$ at 13\tev, referred to as the {Run~1} and {Run~2} data samples, respectively.
In order to avoid experimenter's bias, the data in a mass window within $\pm 40\mevcc$ of the known mass of the \Bs meson~\cite{PDG2024}, corresponding to approximately twice the resolution of the signal decay, were not examined until the full procedure had been finalised.

The LHCb detector~\cite{LHCb-DP-2008-001,LHCb-DP-2014-002} is a single-arm forward spectrometer covering the pseudorapidity range $2 < \eta < 5$.
Detector elements include: a silicon-strip vertex detector (VELO) surrounding the $pp$ interaction region~\cite{LHCb-DP-2014-001}; a tracking system, consisting of detectors upstream and downstream of a dipole magnet with a bending power of about $4{\mathrm{\,T\,m}}$, that provides a measurement of the momentum, $p$, of charged particles~\cite{LHCb-DP-2013-003,LHCb-DP-2017-001}; two ring-imaging Cherenkov (RICH) detectors that are able to discriminate between different species of charged hadrons~\cite{LHCb-DP-2012-003}; and a calorimeter system able to identify and measure the energy of electrons and photons~\cite{LHCb-DP-2020-001}.

The online event selection is performed by a trigger~\cite{LHCb-DP-2012-004,LHCb-DP-2019-001}.
At the hardware trigger stage, events are required to have a muon with high transverse momentum (\pt) or a hadron, photon or electron with high transverse energy (\et) in the calorimeters.
The software trigger performs a full event reconstruction and requires that the signal decay candidates contain a secondary vertex consistent with the decay of a \bquark hadron formed of either multiple tracks~\cite{BBDT,LHCb-PROC-2015-018} or two high-\pt tracks alongside a high-\et photon~\cite{LHCb-PUB-2012-002}.

Simulation is required to model the effects of the detector resolution and acceptance, and the imposed selection requirements.
The $pp$ collisions are generated using \pythia~\cite{Sjostrand:2007gs,*Sjostrand:2006za} with a specific \lhcb configuration~\cite{LHCb-PROC-2010-056}.
Decays of unstable particles are described by \evtgen~\cite{Lange:2001uf}, in which final-state radiation is generated using \photos~\cite{davidson2015photos}.
The interaction of the generated particles with the detector, and its response, are implemented using the \geant toolkit~\cite{Allison:2006ve, *Agostinelli:2002hh} as described in Ref.~\cite{LHCb-PROC-2011-006}.
The simulated samples of \Bd, \Bs and \Lb decays are corrected to account for data-simulation differences in the \bquark-hadron production kinematics and detector occupancy using data samples enriched in \bdkpig, \bskkg, and \lbpkg decays, respectively.
Particle-identification efficiencies are calibrated using dedicated control samples~\cite{LHCb-DP-2018-001}.

Only photons that convert through interactions with the detector material into an electron-positron pair, referred to as \emph{converted photons}~\cite{LHCb-PAPER-2013-028,LHCb-PAPER-2014-040,LHCb-PAPER-2015-044}, are used in this search.
This improves the mass resolution of the \bquark-hadron candidate by a factor of three with respect to candidates formed using photons reconstructed as clusters in the electromagnetic calorimeter (ECAL), reducing the background from the \bdkpig decay. Furthermore, the photon trajectory can be matched to the position of the $\Km\pip$ combination vertex to reduce background from random combinations of photons and hadrons.
Electron candidates are identified among high-\pt tracks that are associated with clusters in the ECAL.
To mitigate loss in resolution from bremsstrahlung before the magnet, electrons are rejected if an ECAL cluster, identifiable as a photon with $\et>75\mev$, is found at a position consistent with the extrapolation of the electron trajectory.
Converted photons are reconstructed in two categories: those that convert early enough that the electrons are reconstructed using the full tracking system (\emph{long} tracks); and those that convert further downstream, where no track segments are formed in the VELO (\emph{downstream} tracks).
To reject background from random combinations, the two electron candidates are required to originate from a common vertex, and the mass of the dielectron must be less than \mbox{50\mevcc}.
The photon candidate must have $\pt>500\mevc$ and its vertex must be significantly displaced from the primary vertex~(PV) that is most consistent with the flight direction of the \B candidate, referred to as the associated PV.

To evaluate the contamination to the \bdskpig signal decays due to particle misidentification, \bskkg and \lbpkg decays are also analysed.
All of these decay candidates are constructed by combining two high-\pt tracks incompatible with originating from any PV and identified using information from the RICH system as pions, kaons or protons, and requiring that they form a common vertex.
The photon candidate is combined with the dihadron pair consistent with originating from the same vertex to form decay candidates that are consistent with originating from a PV.
The dihadron masses are required to be in the ranges \mbox{$796<m(\kpi)<1800\mevcc$},  \mbox{$1000<m(\Kp\Km)<1800\mevcc$} and \mbox{$1400<m(p\Km)<2500\mevcc$}.
To reject backgrounds from decays involving a \piz meson misidentified as a photon, such as the \decay{\Bzb}{\left[\pip\piz\right]_{\rhop}\Km} decay, the mass of the combination of the photon assigned the \piz mass hypothesis with one hadron is required to be greater than $2000\mevcc$.
Momentum resolution is improved by applying a kinematic fit~\cite{Hulsbergen:2005pu}, in which all final-state particles are constrained to originate from the corresponding decay vertex, and the \mbox{$b$-hadron} candidate is constrained to originate from the associated PV.
The mass resolution is further improved by requiring the mass of downstream photon candidates to be below $40\mevcc$ and, for candidates containing long electron tracks, the uncertainty on the \bquark-hadron candidate mass to be less than $20\mevcc$. These requirements are approximately 85\% and 90\% efficient on signal decays, respectively.

The data are analysed separately for the Run~1 and 2 datasets, long and downstream electrons, and low (\mkpilow) and high (\mkpihigh) dihadron mass windows.
Separate boosted decision tree~(BDT) classifiers~\cite{Breiman,AdaBoost,Hocker:2007ht,*TMVA4} are trained for each of these categories to further reject background from random combinations of particles.
The classifiers are trained using simulated \mbox{\decay{\Bs}{\Kstarzbv\gamma}} decays for the signal and \bdskpig candidates in data with masses above $5580\mevcc$ for the background sample.
The classifiers exploit kinematic and topological properties of the decay as well as, for long electron tracks, the amount of charge deposited in the VELO that is larger for converted photons where the two electron tracks overlap, and their compatibility with the electron hypothesis using information from the RICH and calorimeter systems.
The requirement on the classifier in the \kpig final state is optimised simultaneously with requirements on the kaon and pion-identification probabilities to maximise the figure of merit \mbox{$\varepsilon_{\rm sig}/(3/2 + \sqrt{N_{\rm bkg}})$}~\cite{Punzi:2003bu} for the purpose of finding evidence for this decay.
Here, $\varepsilon_{\rm sig}$ denotes the efficiency of the selection requirements determined from simulation, and $N_{\rm bkg}$ is the estimated number of candidates from all processes except the signal decay within a mass window corresponding to four times the signal mass resolution, centred on its peak position.
The optimal particle identification and BDT requirements correspond to a combinatorial background rejection of at least 95\% and a signal efficiency of 55--98\%, where less efficient requirements are necessary for data in \mkpihigh where signal purity is lower.
The same BDT requirement is applied to the \kkg and \pkg final states without any additional particle-identification criteria.

The best sensitivity to the signal decay is obtained using downstream electrons, which have a smaller probability of bremsstrahlung and therefore better momentum resolution, and in the low-mass window occupied by the \Kstarzv resonance that offers higher purity.
Of the eight available subsets of data, only those with the highest signal-to-background purity in data and mass resolution of the normalisation decay are analysed: low-\mkpi with downstream electrons in Run~1 data; both low- and high-\mkpi with downstream electrons in Run~2 data; and low-\mkpi with long track electrons in Run~2 data. The chosen datasets, established prior to examination of the signal region, each provide anexpected signal significance of approximately 1.0 standard deviations or higher assuming the predicted branching fraction; the excluded datasets provide expected significances below 0.3 standard deviations.
Figure~\ref{fig:datafit} shows the mass distributions of \kpig candidates passing the selection requirements in the datasets with best sensitivity.
The signal decay yields are extracted through an extended unbinned maximum-likelihood fit to these data samples.

The \bdskpig signal decays are modelled using Johnson $S_U$ (JSU) functions featuring power-law tails on both sides of the peak~\cite{JohnsonSU}.
For candidates where the downstream tracks are not matched to the correct hits in the tracking detectors, the mass distribution is shifted towards lower values.
To account for this effect, a modified Gaussian function with power-law tails on both sides (double-sided Crystal Ball, DSCB)~\cite{Skwarnicki:1986xj} is added to the JSU function.
All shape parameters are fixed from simulation, except for the relative fraction of the two components and deviations of the overall width and peak position from simulation. These are unconstrained and shared between the \Bd and \bskpig decay models.

Partially reconstructed \mbox{\decay{\B}{\Km\pip\pi(\pi)\gamma}} decays are modelled with ARGUS functions~\cite{ARGUS:1990hfq} convolved with the signal model.
The endpoints of the ARGUS functions are fixed to the peak position of the signal model reduced by the mass of the missing pions.
The other parameters are fixed from simulation, except for the model for two missing pions where the power parameter is free to vary in the fit to data.
The yields corresponding to partially reconstructed \Bd and \Bp meson decays are free to vary in the fit to data.
To estimate the yields of partially reconstructed \Bs meson decays, the \mbox{\decay{\B}{\Km\pip\pi(\pi)\gamma}} decay yields are scaled by the ratio of \Bs to \B meson production cross-sections~\cite{LHCb-PAPER-2020-046} and the branching fraction ratio estimated by applying \grpsuthree flavour symmetry while accounting for dominant sources of \grpsuthree breaking.

The \mbox{\decay{\BdorBsorbar}{\kpi\piz}} and \mbox{\decay{\BdorBsorbar}{\kpi\eta}} decays, where one of the two photons from the \piz or $\eta$ decay is not reconstructed, are similarly modelled using ARGUS functions convolved with the signal model.
The ARGUS endpoint is set to the peak position of the signal model and the shape parameters are fixed from simulation.
The yields of such components are fixed relative to that of the \bdkpig decay using selection efficiencies from simulation and measured branching fractions, or predictions where measurements are not available~\cite{BaBar:2011vfx,Zhou:2023lbc,Wang:2020saq,PDG2024}.
The yields of \mbox{\decay{\Bs}{K^{*0}_J\eta}} decays \mbox{($J=0,2$)}, where neither measurements nor predictions are available, are estimated similarly to the yield of \mbox{\decay{\Bs}{\Km\pip\pi(\pi)\gamma}} decays.

The \bskkg, \lbpkg and \mbox{\decay{\Bd}{\pip\pim\gamma}} decays, where one or both hadrons are misidentified, are modelled using DSCB functions with shape parameters fixed from simulation.
The yield of \mbox{\decay{\Bd}{\pip\pim\gamma}} decays is fixed relative to the yield of \bdkpig decays using efficiencies from simulation and the known \mbox{\decay{\Bd}{\rho(770)^0\gamma}} branching fraction~\cite{LHCb-PAPER-2025-017}, neglecting contributions from higher-mass resonances.
The yields of the other misidentified decays are determined using data selected in the \kkg and \pkg final states, as described in the End Matter.



Finally, \mbox{\decay{\BdorBsorbar}{\Kstarzbv\ep\en}} and misidentified \mbox{\decay{\BdorBsorbar}{\Kstarzbv\pip\pim}} decays are modelled using a JSU and a Gaussian function, respectively.
The \Bzb decay yields are fixed relative to that of the \bdkpig decay using efficiencies from simulation and measured branching fractions~\cite{Belle:2009zue,BaBar:2008jdv,BaBar:2007nlh,PDG2024}; the yields of the corresponding \Bs decays are further scaled using the same method as for \mbox{\decay{\Bs}{\Km\pip\pi(\pi)\gamma}} decays.
The contamination from \mbox{\decay{\BdorBsorbar}{\Kstarzbv\ep\en}} and \mbox{\decay{\BdorBsorbar}{\Kstarzbv\pip\pim}} decays are negligible for downstream electrons.
Background from random combinations of particles is modelled using a first-order polynomial function with free slope and yield.

Systematic uncertainties on the signal yield are summarised in Table~\ref{tab:yieldsys}.
Systematic uncertainties on the yield of the \bdkpig decay are calculated similarly.
The systematic uncertainty associated with the statistical uncertainty of the \bskkg and \lbpkg decay yields is evaluated as the reduction in the uncertainty of the \bskpig decay yield when those contributions are fixed.
All other uncertainties are evaluated from pseudoexperiments generated with an alternative model, taking as the uncertainty the average absolute difference in yield obtained when fitting the baseline model compared to the alternative model.
The alternative models are as follows:
For \bdskpig and \mbox{\decay{\BdorBsorbar}{\Kstarzbv\ep\en}} decays, and the resolution model for partially reconstructed decays, a different width parameter is used for each side of the DSCB function and the JSU function is replaced by a DSCB function.
For long-track electrons, the model is evaluated using only reconstructed tracks matched to the correct hits in the tracking detectors.
For candidates with downstream electrons, the JSU function is evaluated using only candidates where the photon converts downstream of the VELO and the tracks are matched to the correct hits in the tracking detectors, while the DSCB function is evaluated with the remaining candidates.
The model for partially reconstructed \mbox{\decay{\B}{\Km\pip\pi\gamma}} decays is evaluated from simulated \mbox{\decay{\Bp}{\Kstar(892)^0 \pip\gamma}} decays.
Contributions from decays with misidentified hadrons and \mbox{\decay{\BdorBsorbar}{\Kstarzbv\pip\pim}} decays are alternatively modelled using bifurcated Gaussian and symmetric DSCB functions, respectively.
The combinatorial background is modelled with an exponential function.
Branching fractions and \bquark-hadron masses are varied randomly according to their uncertainties in each pseudoexperiment.
Where assumptions are made on branching fractions in the absence of predictions or measurements, a 100\% uncertainty is assumed.
The contamination from \mbox{\decay{\Bd}{\rho(770)^0\gamma}} decays for \mkpihigh is doubled, and a 100\% uncertainty is assigned, to account for missing contributions from heavier resonances.
Shape parameters and efficiencies are varied randomly according to their covariance matrices.
The uncertainty on the hadron-misidentification corrections includes the size of the calibration sample as well as possible biases introduced by the background subtraction method and variations of the corrections within the phase-space bins in which they are derived.
Other simulation corrections, including those related to electron identification, are small and therefore removed.

A yield of \mbox{$38\pm18$} \bskpig decays is measured, where the uncertainty includes both statistical and systematic sources, of which \mbox{$32\pm11$} correspond to the Run~2 downstream low-\mkpi dataset.
Pseudoexperiments show that the probability that background processes can produce a mass distribution that is less compatible with the background-only model than the data, including both statistical and systematic sources of uncertainty, is \mbox{$2.8\times10^{-4}$}, corresponding to a significance of 3.5 standard deviations.

The signal branching fraction is measured relative to that of the \bdkpig decay as
\begin{equation*}
\mathcal{R} \equiv \frac{\BF(\bskpig)}{\BF(\bdkpig)} = \frac{N(\bskpig)}{N(\bdkpig)} \frac{\varepsilon(\bdkpig)}{\varepsilon(\bskpig)} / \fsfd,
\end{equation*}
where $N$ are the yields, $\varepsilon$ are the efficiencies, and \fsfd is the ratio of production cross-sections evaluated by weighting the \pt-dependent measurement~\cite{LHCb-PAPER-2020-046} over the simulated \pt distribution of \bdkpig decays.
The ratio of the efficiencies is consistent with unity for all datasets.
A correction of 1--8\% is applied for a bias in the measured branching fraction arising from the fit method. It is estimated as the average fit bias observed in pseudoexperiments generated with $\mathcal{R}$ equal to its predicted value.

Systematic uncertainties applied on the normalisation of $\mathcal{R}$ are shown in Table~\ref{tab:normsys}.
The uncertainty on the fit bias is the quadrature sum of its statistical uncertainty, the difference in bias between two methods of determining the average, and the variation in the bias value when pseudodatasets are generated with $\mathcal{R}$ equal to its measured value.
The uncertainty on the \BdorBs lifetimes~\cite{HFLAV23} and from the simulation sample size are propagated to uncertainties on the efficiency ratio.
The uncertainties on the measurement of \fsfd are propagated to the weighted average value.
Correlations between measurements from each dataset arising from the use of common inputs for \BdorBs lifetimes and \fsfd are considered.

The measured branching fraction ratio is found to be consistent ($p$-value greater than 0.05) between data corresponding to different \mkpi windows, electron track types, data-taking periods and magnet polarities, and under changes in the selection requirements on the BDT classifier, hadron-identification probabilities, and variables that impact the mass resolution.
A simultaneous fit to all datasets with \mkpilow, with a common value of $\mathcal{R}$, results in the measurement
\begin{equation*}
    \mathcal{R}_{\rm low }=(3.7\pm1.2\pm0.4)\times10^{-2} \quad\text{for}\quad \mkpilow,
\end{equation*}
where the first uncertainty is statistical and the second systematic, which is in excellent agreement with the SM prediction.
Additionally, the ratio
\begin{equation*}
    \mathcal{R}_{\rm high}=(0.2\pm2.7\pm1.3)\times10^{-2} \quad\text{for}\quad \mkpihigh,
\end{equation*}
is measured. Correlations between the two measurements are negligible.

In summary, a search for the rare \bskpig decay is performed using $pp$ collision data corresponding to an integrated luminosity of 9\invfb.
An excess of events is found with a significance of 3.5 standard deviations, which constitutes the first evidence for this decay.
Updated theoretical inputs will permit a test of the SM using the measured branching fraction ratio.
Large improvements in precision are anticipated with the new dataset being collected by the upgraded LHCb detector~\cite{LHCb-DP-2022-002}.

\begin{abstract}
  \noindent
The first search for the $B_s^0\rightarrow K^-\pi^+\gamma$ decay in the range $796<m(K^-\pi^+)<1800\,\text{Me\kern -0.1em V\!/}c^2$ is performed using data from proton-proton collisions collected by the LHCb experiment at centre-of-mass energies of 7, 8, and 13~TeV, corresponding to an integrated luminosity
of 9~fb$^{-1}$.
The photons are reconstructed through their conversion into an electron-positron pair, which significantly improves the mass resolution of the reconstructed decays with respect to decays with an unconverted photon.
A signal excess with a significance of 3.5 standard deviations is measured, constituting the first experimental evidence for this decay.
In the range $796<m(K^-\pi^+)<996\,\text{Me\kern -0.1em V\!/}c^2$, the ratio $\mathcal R$ between the branching fractions of the signal decay and the favoured $\kern 0.18em\overline{\kern -0.18em B}{}^0\rightarrow K^- \pi^+\gamma$ decay is measured to be \mbox{${\mathcal R} = (3.7\pm1.2\pm0.4)\times10^{-2}$} where the first uncertainty is statistical and the second is systematic.
This measurement is consistent with the value predicted in the Standard Model.
In the range $996<m(K^-\pi^+)<1800\,\text{Me\kern -0.1em V\!/}c^2$, the ratio \mbox{${\mathcal R} = (0.2\pm2.7\pm1.3)\times10^{-2}$} is measured.

\end{abstract}
